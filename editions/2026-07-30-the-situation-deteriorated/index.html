There is a different kind of company called a public benefit corporation (PBC) —one step in the financial rhyme. A PBC’s directors are allowed to balance money for shareholders with the company’s public purpose and the interests of people affected by the company —then the consequence lands right on time.

People wondered: if a PBC puts itself up for sale, does it still have to take the highest cash offer —as the risks and rewards come to light. The Delaware Court of Chancery said no — a PBC does not have to sell to the highest bidder because the law requires directors to balance shareholder money against the company’s public purpose —and the numbers help set the tale right.
