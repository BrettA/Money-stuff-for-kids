AI-Backed Securities

STORY 1
IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Securitization

Wall Street Would Like to Put the AI Data Center in a Box

AI needs chips, electricity and giant buildings full of computers. All of this costs an enormous amount of money.

Suppose an investment firm builds a data center and rents it to an AI company. Those future rent payments are valuable.

Wall Street has a favorite trick: put things that produce money into a financial box, then sell investors different pieces of the money coming out.

The safest pieces get paid first. Riskier pieces wait longer and can earn more. This is called securitization.

Now AI data centers are so enormous that financiers are asking whether their loans can go into the box too.

If the AI boom keeps booming, everyone gets paid and the box looks brilliant. If not, Wall Street will at least have built a very sophisticated box.

STORY 2
IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Truth Social API

The News That Was Worth More Because It Was Faster

Important government news can move stock prices very quickly, so traders would love to receive it before everyone else.

For computer traders, even a few milliseconds can matter.

Truth Social was involved in a plan for a very fast feed of government announcements that could move markets.

The product is basically: here is the same sentence everyone will read, except your computer gets it first.

That can be valuable. It also creates an awkward question: when does “fast news service” become “some traders get important government information before everyone else”?

Sometimes a millisecond is barely enough time to blink. Sometimes a millisecond is a product.

STORY 3
IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Gambling emergency

The Emergency About Keeping the Betting Market Open

Kalshi trades prediction contracts, including contracts about sports. This has created a fight over what Kalshi actually is.

States normally regulate sports gambling. Kalshi says it is a federally regulated financial exchange, even when the thing being traded is a sports outcome.

During one legal fight, the possibility that some Kalshi trading might stop was treated as an urgent market issue.

This is funny because the emergency was not “a bank is collapsing.” It was closer to “people might temporarily be unable to trade contracts about sports.”

But the label matters enormously: financial market or sports betting? Sometimes the most valuable part of a financial product is the category everyone agrees to put it in.

STORY 4
IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Musical earnings calls

How Wall Street Helped Make an AI Music Company

Wall Street hires lots of mathematicians, physicists and computer scientists because finance has hard problems involving data, speech and machine learning.

Some people who worked on machine-learning systems for financial audio later helped build Suno, the AI music company.

So the career path goes: study science, work in finance, teach computers about sound, help build a computer that makes songs.

Nobody planning a career in elementary school would draw the arrows in that order.

But technology often spreads this way. One industry pays to solve a difficult problem, and later the solution wanders off and does something much stranger.
