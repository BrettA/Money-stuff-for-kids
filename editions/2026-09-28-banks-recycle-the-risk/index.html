IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Structured notes

The Banks and the Thorny Notes

Banks sold structured notes with stock options tucked inside— Their payouts followed special rules as markets dipped or climbed. Customers bought more and more; sales more than doubled in size, But the notes could leave their makers with a thorny surprise.

Bank of America, BNP Paribas, Citigroup too, Societe Generale and UBS tried something new: They sold each note to a customer, then paid behind the scene For a matching hedge that helped to keep their own books clean.

Citadel Securities and Jane Street joined the crew; Millennium Management and Optiver did too. These firms took back-to-back hedges—sometimes a quarter or more— Of the risk that banks once kept behind their own front door.

“Two years ago, this barely happened,” UBS’s Thibaut Delahaye said. Now recycling risk frees room for more notes to spread. But Goldman Sachs and JP Morgan were wary of the trend: If banks hand off the danger, what special thing do they vend?

The risk supermarket still filled every customer’s sack— Then paid another shop to take the prickly risk out back.

What happened? Banks more than doubled sales of equity-linked structured notes, then increasingly bought back-to-back hedges from firms including Citadel Securities, Jane Street, Millennium Management, and Optiver to transfer as much as a quarter of the risk, and sometimes more. Goldman Sachs and JP Morgan were reported to be less active and more skeptical of this approach.

Nvidia’s Great AI Money Loop

The AI boom needs power plants, data centers, and loans, With racks full of servers and chips of their own. Builders raise money today, expecting one day That future AI sales will make borrowing pay.

SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI may sell shares; Alphabet may raise equity to fund its affairs. While much of the market asks investors for more, Nvidia already has cash by the score.

Its GPUs fill the new data-center lanes, With a 75% gross margin on sales and gains. That means from each hundred in chip revenue earned, Seventy-five dollars remain before other costs are returned.

Bloomberg’s Amy Thomson reported the plan: CEO Jensen Huang’s company added a record $150 billion buyback authorization—and Nvidia now has $235 billion approved to buy shares, With completion expected through fiscal 2028 in its corporate affairs.

Yet six weeks before, Nvidia had offered a hand To help third-party investors fund AI’s expansion as planned: More than $500 billion for infrastructure over time— Then Nvidia announced its own cash mountain’s climb.

So everyone else borrows on hoped-for AI pay, And Nvidia helps outside capital flow their way. They spend it on Nvidia chips for each rack— Then Nvidia pays its own shareholders back!

What happened? Nvidia helped establish financing platforms intended to mobilize more than $500 billion of third-party capital for AI infrastructure, whose builders buy Nvidia chips. Nvidia separately authorized $235 billion of remaining share buybacks through fiscal 2028, returning its chip-sale cash to shareholders instead of using that cash to finance the infrastructure itself.

The Boss of His Bosses

At Automattic, maker of WordPress, founder Matt held sway; Mullenweg was CEO, with eighty-four percent in shares that day. The board could remove him from the chief executive’s chair, But firing the chief could not make his stock disappear.

TechCrunch reported: the board put Matt on leave against his will; One person guessed his Burning Man trip helped them time it well. Mark Davies, the CFO, took charge in Matt’s stead; For thirty-three hours and twenty minutes, he led.

But Matt seemingly declined to depart as they had planned; He booted other admins from Slack and said he was in command. Then Matt pushed out the board and restored himself as chief; The directors’ time in charge had been remarkably brief.

In military coups, seize radio and TV in the attack; In a corporate coup, it helps to be the admin of Slack. Before the window closed, two exit deals were signed: Mark Davies and legal chief Andy Missan approved each other’s kind.

Each deal was generous, effective September 10— A softer way to land if they were pushed outside again. The board had tried to bench the chief, but ownership won through: Matt was boss of all his bosses—and the company’s Slack crew.

What happened? Automattic’s board put founder and 84% shareholder Matt Mullenweg on leave as CEO, but he retained voting control, pushed out the board, and restored himself after 33 hours and 20 minutes. During that window, interim CEO Mark Davies and chief legal officer Andy Missan signed each other’s generous severance agreements, while Mullenweg used his Slack-admin access to boot other admins.

IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Agentic sports gambling

The Bots That Bet—But Not on Sports Yet

Public called itself the first agentic brokerage around, where AI helpers can trade without customers being bound to click every button or choose every bet— the agent follows its rules, again and again, without sweat.

Through Kalshi, Public members can trade contracts on events: Will prices climb? Will elections bring market-moving developments? If the predicted event occurs, the contract may pay; if the prediction is wrong, its value can fade away.

The categories named crypto, climate, politics, and tech, plus commodities, companies, indices—quite a deck. The agents might even use an event-price clue to decide what a stock or a bond ought to do.

But sports were not listed. No Jets game appeared. No robot chose teams while their human just cheered. Still, one day later, OpenWorlds joined Polymarket’s floor, bringing personal trading agents through another prediction-market door.

So how long until bots pick the spread and the score, placing hundreds of wagers while fans watch no more? Perhaps “two weeks, tops,” was the joking forecast— a future where even the slot lever pulls itself fast.

What happened? Public announced AI agents that can trade Kalshi prediction-market contracts, and OpenWorlds announced a partnership bringing personal trading agents to Polymarket. The announced categories did not include sports, but the joke was that automated sports gambling seemed only about two weeks away.
