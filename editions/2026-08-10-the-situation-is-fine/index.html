STORY 1
IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Situational Awareness

The Hedge Fund That Almost Blew Up and Became More Interesting

Leopold Aschenbrenner is extremely excited about AI. He started a hedge fund called Situational Awareness and made lots of investments based on one big idea: AI is going to become incredibly important.

The fund bought companies AI might help and bet against companies AI might hurt. It looked like many bets. Then many started losing at once.

Imagine packing four pairs of shorts, two swimsuits and six pairs of sunglasses. You packed many things, but really you made one bet: it will be hot.

Situational Awareness had done something similar with AI, and it had borrowed money to make the bets bigger. When the trades went wrong, it lost a huge amount and had to sell investments.

On Wall Street, nearly blowing up your hedge fund is evidence something went wrong. In Silicon Valley, there is another possible reaction: Wow. This guy REALLY believes in AI.

Wall Street asks, “How much did you lose?” Silicon Valley sometimes asks, “Yes, but how enormous was the dream?”

STORY 2
IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Intraday momentum

The Trade That Says 'It Is Going Up, So Buy It'

There are complicated ways to choose investments. Momentum has a wonderfully simple summary: this thing is going up, so maybe it will keep going up.

Intraday momentum tries to catch that movement inside one trading day. A computer spots something running upward, buys it, and hopes to get out before the running stops.

This sounds a bit like chasing someone because they are already running.

Which it is.

But if prices do it often enough, people will build extremely serious mathematical systems for doing the chasing.

STORY 3
IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Tick sizes

How Finance Made Everyone Care About One Penny

Prediction-market contracts often cost less than one dollar, so a penny can be a surprisingly big deal.

If a contract costs 10 cents, paying 11 cents is about 10% more. For a $200 stock, a one-cent difference is almost nothing.

Markets have a smallest allowed price step, called a tick. On many prediction contracts it is one cent.

That can be good for traders who stand between buyers and sellers and earn the difference.

A penny is normally the coin you ignore on the sidewalk. Finance has found a way to make extremely serious adults care deeply about it.

STORY 4
IN MATT'S ARTICLE: Private equity boyfriends

Private Equity's Extremely Glamorous Pest-Control Era

Private-equity firms buy companies, try to improve them and later sell them. The companies do not have to be glamorous.

They might make industrial parts, run dental offices or kill bugs. A useful pest-control business can make perfectly good money even if nobody makes a movie about it.

Private-equity people themselves, however, have somehow become glamorous in certain corners of culture. Articles discuss them as desirable boyfriends. Celebrities date financiers.

So there is a pleasing mismatch.

By day: an extremely serious meeting about the profit margins of regional pest-control companies.

By night: apparently, heartthrob.
